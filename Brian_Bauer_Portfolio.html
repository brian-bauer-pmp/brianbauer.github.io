Software product · Managed Services

Cutting Managed Services deployment from 33 days to 6

As Senior Technical Program Manager and Acting Product Manager, I led Operations, Product, Development Engineering, and Testing Engineering in removing duplicated steps, clarifying ownership, and closing gaps in information gathering, documentation, tools, automation, and testing.

Result: Reduced a deployment cycle from 33 days to 6. Automation removed 21 days; clearer ownership and parallel runbook steps removed another 6.
Challenge, decisions, and delivery steps

My role: Established the baseline, brought stakeholders together, clarified accountability, and coordinated process, tooling, testing, and automation changes. I also provided weekly updates to senior staff.

Key decision: Replace duplicated sequential work and broad testing bottlenecks with clear ownership, reusable information templates, targeted automation, and a more modular testing framework aligned with just-in-time development.

  1. Set the baseline. Defined the current deployment cycle at 33 days.
  2. Map the process. Worked with stakeholders to map the work and find opportunities, duplication, and handoff gaps.
  3. Clarify ownership. Created a RACI with stakeholders to assign roles, actions, and work to the right teams.
  4. Standardize inputs. Created information-gathering templates and had Operations verify them.
  5. Close process and tool gaps. Reviewed processes, tools, and automations with Product and Development Engineering; coordinated fixes and updates, including documentation and troubleshooting guides.
  6. Test readiness. Ran tabletop exercises to validate the updated process before release.
  7. Modularize testing. Developed a more modular testing framework and connected it to just-in-time development to reduce testing time.
  8. Automate delivery steps. Worked with Product, Development Engineering, and Testing Engineering to develop and release automations.
  9. Keep leaders aligned. Shared weekly progress updates with senior staff.

Cycle-time breakdown: Automation removed 21 days, and parallel runbook steps with named owners removed 6 days from the original 33-day cycle.

Executive reporting · Automation

Making program status available on demand

Created four burndown reports with forecasting for four program groups, consolidating five Jira sources—three on-premises and two cloud-based—for executive, director, and core-team reviews.

Outcome: Replaced reporting that could take up to a week with consistent, on-demand updates in about four minutes after downloading source files, freeing at least nine people from recurring manual reporting work.
Challenge, decisions, and delivery steps

Challenge. As release approached, four program groups needed more frequent, consistent updates. Manual Jira pulls, stakeholder checks, data cleanup, team-specific breakouts, and final reconciliation could stretch reporting to a week. Leaders sometimes received conflicting metrics while the deadline was approaching.

  1. Align the data. Took ownership of reporting and worked with stakeholders to validate measures, standardize definitions across all five Jira sources, and secure sign-off.
  2. Build for each audience. Created four burndown reports for executive, director, and core-team reviews, with iteration and release views and breakouts for Product, Architecture, Development, and Test. Testing results distinguished bugs and defects from enhancements.
  3. Automate reporting. Used Devin as an AI coding tool to build a reusable program that reconciled differences across source formats, applied the agreed metrics consistently, and generated forecasts.
  4. Enable reuse. Shared the program with colleagues, along with prerequisites, instructions, and troubleshooting guidance.

Downloading the source files took about three minutes; generating the reports averaged one minute. With metric definitions agreed in advance, teams could provide updates on demand as release activity intensified.

Reliability · Incident response

Recovering 58,000+ servers after seven outages

A month into my Senior Advisor role, I took ownership of a four-month recovery after seven power-related outages affected the fleet. I coordinated customer, site engineering, hardware engineering, and logistics partners to restore 58,000+ servers.

Outcome: Traced drive failures to the interaction of workload, drive type, and firmware; secured interim firmware and established controlled replacement and testing while the final fix was validated.
Recovery approach and reliability work
  • Investigated each outage with onsite engineering and external partners. Causes included utility substation failure, failover and power-transfer problems, and a field-work error; defined infrastructure remediation and prevention measures.
  • Worked with hardware engineering and the customer to trace abnormal drive fallout to the interaction of workload, drive type, and firmware.
  • Used six months of replacement data, stock on hand, and emergency orders to forecast supply needs; coordinated cross-site drive deliveries.
  • Tracked replacement drives by server and audited removed drives before wipe and reuse or destruction.
  • Worked with leadership to secure temporary staffing and overtime; built an assembly-line process for drive replacement, server testing, firmware updates, and in-rack testing.
  • Provided daily recovery status and forecasts to the customer and director.

Separately, root-cause work across 1.5M+ drives helped reduce failure rates by 40% against baseline.
